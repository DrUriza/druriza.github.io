VR5 — INTELLIGENCE & ORCHESTRATION

Intelligence for interaction, explanation, and coordination.

VR5 is an intelligence and orchestration layer that interacts with the existing TradELATIN architecture. It may consult, coordinate, explain, contextualize and orchestrate capabilities across VR1–VR4.

VR5 coordinates intelligence. VR4 retains execution authority.

Role in the architecture

A transversal intelligence layer

VR5 does not replace VR1–VR4. Observations remain in VR1, structural representations in VR2, predictive information in VR3, and decisions and controlled execution in VR4.

VR5 is designed to make these capabilities accessible through supervised interaction, explanation, context and coordination. Its role is broader than a fifth serial processing stage.

Interaction model

A user request enters VR5. Bounded requests may query or coordinate VR1, VR2, VR3 and VR4 under each layer’s interfaces and permissions.

Functional pipeline: VR1 → VR2 → VR3 → VR4

VR5 interacts with every layer. Execution authorization and execution-state transitions remain in VR4.

  • VR1 — consult observations and provenance.
  • VR2 — inspect structural states and representation scope.
  • VR3 — explain predictive outputs, uncertainty and validation evidence.
  • VR4 — consult decision and execution state; changes remain subject to VR4 authorization and risk controls.
Authority boundary

LLM ≠ Trading Authority

VR5 can query, explain, coordinate and recommend. Execution authority, risk controls and execution-state transitions remain in VR4.

Prediction ≠ Execution Authorization

Human supervision and explicit VR4 authorization are required at sensitive boundaries. An orchestration request cannot bypass limits, permissions or operating-mode gates.

Human supervision

Escalation before sensitive actions

VR5 should expose uncertainty and missing evidence, preserve traceability and escalate sensitive actions rather than silently acquiring execution authority.

  • Explicit permissions and bounded tool access.
  • Human approval at sensitive workflow boundaries.
  • Inspectible source context, model outputs and request history.
  • Safe failure, stop conditions and intervention paths.
  • No bypass of VR4 risk limits or execution-mode authorization.
