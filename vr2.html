VR2 · STRUCTURE

Mathematical representation of market structure

TradELATIN VR2 investigates mathematical representations capable of transforming synchronized VR1 observations into reproducible descriptions of market structure, geometry, topology, scale, and transition.

VR2 studies structure. It does not predict market direction, generate trading signals, or execute trades.

Layer purpose

From observation to structural description

VR2 receives structured VR1 observations and studies how reproducible mathematical transformations can represent them.

Research lines

Candidate methods under investigation

Listing a method does not assert that it is already implemented or validated.

Normalization and temporal alignment

Comparable timing, scales, missingness, and sampling assumptions.

PCA and SVD

Linear dimensionality reduction and reproducible low-rank representations.

Topological Data Analysis

Shape-oriented investigation of high-dimensional observations.

Persistent homology

Multiscale topological features across filtration values.

Persistence diagrams and images

Stable summaries and vectorized representations of persistence.

Betti numbers

Structural descriptors tracking connected components, loops, and higher-dimensional features.

Mapper representations

Graph-based views of high-dimensional organization.

Wavelet transforms

Continuous and discrete multiscale localization.

Entropy and complexity

Measurements of organization, variability, and structural complexity.

Wasserstein and Bottleneck distances

Distances for comparing topological summaries.

Regimes and transitions

Candidate structural states and reproducible changes between them.

Main questions

What VR2 must establish

  • Which structural properties remain stable across time and market conditions?
  • Which transformations preserve relevant information from heterogeneous VR1 families?
  • How can transitions between structural regimes be detected reproducibly?
  • Which representations are sufficiently stable and interpretable for future predictive evaluation?
  • How sensitive are the representations to sampling, noise, normalization, and parameter selection?

Limits and relationships

Structural separation is not prediction

VR2 depends on versioned VR1 observations and may provide candidates for future VR3 evaluation.

  • VR2 does not make price predictions.
  • VR2 does not produce buy or sell signals.
  • VR2 does not execute orders.
  • Structural separation does not automatically imply predictive usefulness.
  • Every representation must be evaluated before being exposed to VR3.
